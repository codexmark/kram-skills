---
name: hooked-retention-ux
description: Analyze retention and repeat-use loops through trigger, action, reward, and investment while explicitly rejecting manipulative dark patterns.
---
Use when the user asks why people do not return, how to improve engagement, or where a recurring product loop breaks.

1. Define the recurring user value first. Retention without recurring value is not a UX goal.
2. Map **trigger -> action -> reward -> investment** for the target behavior.
3. For each stage identify friction, missing value, timing problems, trust barriers, accessibility barriers, and dependency on notifications or external conditions.
4. Separate healthy product utility from compulsion. Do not recommend deceptive streaks, artificial scarcity, hidden cancellation, infinite-scroll traps, guilt, or exploitative variable rewards.
5. Evaluate whether the requested action is simple enough at the moment of intent.
6. Check whether the reward actually resolves the user's need and whether the user's investment improves future utility rather than merely increasing lock-in.
7. Propose measurable experiments with clear success and harm metrics.
8. Prefer retention caused by usefulness, saved work, accumulated context, mastery, and trust.

Output the weakest stage, evidence/assumptions, recommended experiments, and ethical risks.
