---
name: design-research-and-strategy
description: Structure product/design discovery through users, journeys, jobs, pain points, hypotheses, metrics, and design-ops artifacts before solutioning.
---
Use when the problem is unclear, the user asks for UX research/strategy, or implementation risks solving the wrong problem.

1. Separate evidence from assumptions. Label inferred users, needs, and behaviors as hypotheses until validated.
2. Define the decision the research must support; do not collect artifacts for ceremony.
3. Build only the artifacts that help that decision: stakeholder map, persona/proto-persona, jobs-to-be-done, empathy map, journey, service blueprint, problem statement, opportunity map, or UX metrics.
4. Trace every proposed feature or design intervention back to an observed pain point, constraint, or explicit product goal.
5. Include edge users, accessibility needs, operational staff, administrators, and support roles when relevant.
6. Record unresolved questions and the cheapest way to validate them.
7. Prefer behavioral evidence over preference polling.
8. End with prioritized opportunities, risks, measurable success criteria, and what not to build yet.

Do not fabricate interviews, analytics, or user quotes. Synthetic research must be clearly labeled as hypothesis generation.
